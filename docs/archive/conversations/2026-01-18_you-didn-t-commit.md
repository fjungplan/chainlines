### Planner Response

Great question! The short answer is: **It builds the full group first, then applies the ABC strategies once on the whole group.**

Here is exactly how the logic flows in the current implementation:

1.  **Maximal Grouping First**:
    *   The algorithm picks a seed chain (prioritizing leaves) and runs a Breadth-First Search (BFS) to find **every** chain connected to it via lineage (parents/children).
    *   It continues until it hits a natural boundary (no more connections).
    *   This creates a **Maximal Connected Component**. It does *not* stop at size 2 or 3; if 50 chains are linked, it builds a group of 50 immediately.

2.  **One-Pass Optimization**:
    *   Once this large group is identified, the ABC strategies runs on it **as a whole**:
        *   **Strategy A (Rigid)**: Can this *entire block of 50* move up/down together?
        *   **Strategy B (Swaps)**: Can we swap any pair inside this block of 50?
        *   **Strategy C (Annealing)**: Can we shake this block of 50 to find a better shape?

So it is **not** a gradual "grow-then-optimize-then-grow" cycle. It's a "identify-the-island-then-move-the-island" approach.

### Why this approach?
*   **Stability**: Moving small subgroups might tear them away from the rest of the family. Moving the whole connected block ensures we respect the strongest constraints (lineage) first.
*   **Performance**: Rigid moves on large blocks are actually very cheap (it's one check for the whole block vs. many checks for individual nodes).

Does this align with your intuition for how it should behave?
